# Gear relation

A relation couples two joints: turn one and the other turns with it, by a ratio. A **gear** relation ties the rotations of two revolute (or cylindrical) mates — meshing gears, a belt drive, a pulley pair. A **rack and pinion** relation ties a rotation to the travel of a slider (or cylindrical) mate. The joints keep their own freedom: drag either part, or animate either joint, and both move.

| Relation | Mate A | Mate B | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gear | revolute or cylindrical | revolute or cylindrical | turns of B per turn of A |
| Rack and pinion | revolute or cylindrical (the pinion) | slider or cylindrical (the rack) | travel of B per turn of A |

## In the viewport

1. Mate each gear to its axle with a **Revolute** mate first — a relation couples existing joints.
2. Click **Gear** on the assembly toolbar (or **Rack** for a rack and pinion). The Joints panel switches to picking: click the first joint's row, then the second's. Clicking a part in the viewport offers the joints on that part instead.
3. Set the **Ratio** — how many turns the second joint makes per turn of the first. Two external gears on parallel axes turn opposite ways, so tick **Reverse** for a meshing pair.
4. Drag a gear while the dialog is open: the other one turns with it. Click **Apply**. The relation gets its own row in the **Joints** panel, under the joints it couples.

## Options

```js
relation('gear', pinionAxle, wheelAxle, 0.5)
    .reverse()            // the wheel turns against the pinion's sense
    .name('drive')        // a stable name, unique within the assembly

// A rack: 20 mm pitch radius → 2π·20 ≈ 125.66 mm of travel per pinion turn.
relation('rack-and-pinion', pinionAxle, rackSlide, 125.66);
```

## How the coupling works

Each rotation is measured about its own mate's Z axis, each travel along it, so the sign depends on how the connectors point: a positive ratio moves the second joint in the same sense as the first, **Reverse** the opposite. The relation holds from wherever the parts stand when it is first solved — the file's poses set the mesh phase. Rotate a gear's `insert()` to re-phase the teeth.

A planar mate frees two directions and a spin, so it has no single travel to couple: put a rack on a slider mate. When a solve cannot hold the ratio — one of the coupled joints is held by another mate, say — the relation's row shows a red dot.
