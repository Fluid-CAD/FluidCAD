# Constraints

Constraints describe the relationships your geometry must satisfy — "these two endpoints touch", "this line is horizontal", "this arc is tangent to that line". You draw [primitives](./primitives) at rough guess positions, state the relationships, and the solver moves the geometry until every relationship holds.

```js
import { coincident, horizontal, vertical, parallel, perpendicular,
         tangent, equal, concentric, collinear, midpoint, symmetric,
         fix } from 'fluidcad/constraints';
```

Dimensional constraints (`distance`, `angle`, `radius`, `diameter`) get their own page: [Dimensions](./dimensions).

## The constraint catalog

| Constraint | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `coincident(a, b)` | Two points coincide — or a point lies on a line/arc/circle |
| `horizontal(l)` | The line is horizontal |
| `vertical(l)` | The line is vertical |
| `parallel(a, b, ...)` | Two or more lines are parallel |
| `perpendicular(a, b)` | Two lines are perpendicular |
| `tangent(a, b)` | A line/arc/circle is tangent to another |
| `equal(a, b, ...)` | Equal length (lines) or equal radius (arcs/circles) — two or more entities |
| `concentric(a, b)` | Two arcs/circles share a center |
| `collinear(a, b)` | A line lies along another line (or axis) |
| `midpoint(p, l)` | The point sits at the line's midpoint |
| `symmetric(a, b, l)` | Two points mirror across a line or axis |
| `fix(p [, [x, y]])` | Pins a point (optionally at explicit coordinates) |

Constraints reference geometry through the statement itself (`horizontal(l)`) or through point accessors (`coincident(l.end(), a.start())`).

## The canonical rectangle

The clearest illustration of guesses vs. constraints — four sloppy lines, snapped into an exact 100 × 50 rectangle:

<CodeBlock language="js">{constraintRectangle}</CodeBlock>

![Rectangle solved from rough guesses](/img/docs/sketching/constraint-rectangle.png)

Each corner is pinned by `coincident`, the sides by `horizontal`/`vertical`, the position by `fix`, and the size by two `distance` dimensions. The result is **fully constrained** — zero degrees of freedom — and renders green in the viewport.

## Anchoring: fix

Without an anchor, a perfectly-shaped profile can still slide around the plane as a rigid body. `fix()` pins a point:

```js
fix(b.start())              // pin at its current guess position
fix(b.start(), [0, 0])      // pin at explicit coordinates
```

You can also constrain against the sketch datums instead — see below.

## Tangency

`tangent(a, b)` makes two curved-or-straight entities touch smoothly. Combined with `coincident` at the junction, it produces kink-free profiles:

<CodeBlock language="js">{constraintTangentJunction}</CodeBlock>

![Line–arc junction tangency](/img/docs/sketching/constraint-tangent-junction.png)

The `coincident(l.end(), a.start())` joins the profile; the `tangent(l, a)` forces the arc to leave the line without a corner. The radius dimension then sizes the arc.

For a tangent line touching a circle *somewhere* (not at a shared junction), pair `tangent(l, c)` with `coincident(l.end(), c)` — the point-on-circle form of coincident — to pin the line's endpoint to the tangency point.

## Multiple solutions: guesses pick the branch

Many constraint systems have more than one valid answer — a circle tangent to a line can rest on either side of it. **The guess coordinates choose the branch**: the solver converges to the solution nearest your guesses.

<CodeBlock language="js">{constraintBranch}</CodeBlock>

![Same constraints, two branches](/img/docs/sketching/constraint-branch.png)

Both circles carry identical constraints; only the guessed centers differ, so one solves above the line and one below. Place your guesses on the side you mean — roughly is fine, the correct side is what matters.

A classic use: the two outer tangent lines of a belt run around two pulleys —

<CodeBlock language="js">{constraintBelt}</CodeBlock>

![Belt tangents around two pulleys](/img/docs/sketching/constraint-belt.png)

## Sketch datums

Every sketch carries three implicit fixed entities — `origin()`, `xAxis()`, and `yAxis()` — importable from `fluidcad/core`. They cost nothing and never move; constrain against them instead of fixing arbitrary points:

<CodeBlock language="js">{constraintDatums}</CodeBlock>

![Datum-anchored circles](/img/docs/sketching/constraint-datums.png)

Common patterns:

```js
coincident(c.center(), origin())      // center on the sketch origin
coincident(p, xAxis())                // point on the X axis
collinear(xAxis(), l)                 // line along the X axis
symmetric(a, b, yAxis())              // mirror pair across the Y axis
distance(origin(), c.center(), 40)    // dimension from the origin
```

## Fixed references

`project()` and `intersect()` bring existing 3D geometry into the sketch as **fixed reference entities** — the solver treats them as immovable, and your sketch geometry can be constrained against them (`tangent(bore, l)`, `coincident(p, outline.ref(2))`, …). See [Projection](./projection).

## Reading the solver's verdict

- **Green** geometry is fully constrained — locked by the constraint system.
- **Default-colored** geometry still has freedom; drag it in the viewport and the solver re-solves live, respecting every constraint.
- **Red** geometry is in conflict: the constraints cannot all hold. Remove or adjust one — the diagnostics point at the conflicting statements by file and line.
- **Redundant** constraints (satisfied but adding nothing) are reported too — tidy sketches keep zero.

The DOF count and its consequences are covered in [Dimensions](./dimensions#degrees-of-freedom).
