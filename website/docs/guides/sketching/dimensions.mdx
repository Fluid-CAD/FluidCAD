# Dimensions

Dimensions are constraints with a **value**: they don't just relate two entities, they measure the relationship and hold it at a number. Four statements cover them all:

```js
import { distance, angle, radius, diameter } from 'fluidcad/constraints';
```

| Dimension | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `distance(a, b, value)` | Distance between two points, a point and a line, or a line and a circle/arc |
| `angle(a, b, degrees)` | Angle between two lines |
| `radius(a, value)` | Radius of an arc or circle |
| `diameter(c, value)` | Diameter of a circle |

Dimensions render as measurement labels in the viewport. **Double-click a label** to edit its value in place — the statement in your source updates to match.

## Linear distances

`distance(p1, p2, value)` measures point to point:

![Dimensioned L-profile](/img/docs/sketching/dimension-linear.png)

Note the guesses draw the base 90 long and the rise 40 tall — the dimensions then stretch them to 100 and 45. Combined with `horizontal`/`vertical`, a point-pair distance acts as a width or height; between free points it measures the aligned (straight-line) gap.

## Radial dimensions

`radius()` for arcs, `diameter()` for circles — matching how each is usually annotated in a drawing:

## Angles

`angle(a, b, degrees)` holds the angle between two lines:

<CodeBlock language="js">{dimensionAngle}</CodeBlock>

![30° ramp dimension](/img/docs/sketching/dimension-angle.png)

To dimension a line against the horizontal or vertical, use the sketch datums: `angle(xAxis(), ramp, 30)`.

## Line ↔ circle distances: near side and far side

A distance from a line to a circle or arc measures to the **circumference**, not the center — the near side by default. Chain `.max()` to measure the far side instead:

<CodeBlock language="js">{dimensionFarSide}</CodeBlock>

![Near-side and far-side circumference distances](/img/docs/sketching/dimension-far-side.png)

To measure to the center instead, dimension the center point: `distance(wall, c.center(), 150)`.

## Degrees of freedom

Every sketch entity carries parameters — a line has 4 (two endpoints), a circle 3, an arc 5. Each constraint removes degrees of freedom (DOF). The sketch status readout shows the balance:

- **Under-constrained** — DOF remaining. The geometry rests at your guesses and can still be dragged in the viewport; the solver re-solves live while you drag, honoring every constraint. This is a perfectly fine working state — you don't have to fully constrain every sketch.
- **Fully constrained** — zero DOF. Every entity is locked and renders **green**. This is the goal for production parts: the sketch is completely determined by its dimensions, so edits are predictable.
- **Over-constrained / conflicting** — the diagnostics name the conflicting or redundant statements (by file and line) and the affected geometry renders **red**. Remove the surplus constraint.

A useful habit: constrain the *shape* first (coincident, horizontal, tangent, …), then the *size* (dimensions), then the *position* (`fix` or datum constraints), and watch the DOF counter fall to zero.

## Dimensioning interactively

The sketch toolbar's **Dimension** tool writes these statements for you:

1. Pick an entity — or two. One circle → a diameter; one arc → a radius; one line → its length; two points, a point and a line, two parallel lines, or a line and a circle → a distance; two non-parallel lines → an angle.
2. For point pairs that aren't axis-aligned, move the cursor to choose the measure: horizontal, vertical, or aligned — the preview follows the cursor, and a click places the label.
3. Type the value. The statement is inserted into your source at the right spot, and the solver applies it immediately.

Double-clicking existing geometry works as a shortcut: a circle's edge opens its diameter, an arc its radius, a dimension label its value.
