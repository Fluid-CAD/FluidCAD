# connector()

A connector is a named coordinate frame attached to the part's geometry — an origin, an X direction and a Z normal. Mates in an assembly join connectors to connectors, so the connectors a part declares are its mating interface. Every instance of the part carries the same set. A bolt circle or a hole grid needs only one: declare it on the first hole and [copy it](#copying-a-connector).

## In the viewport

1. Click **Connector** on the toolbar. The Connector dialog docks on the right with its **Source** slot armed.
2. Hover the model. Faces and edges float their anchor candidates — the centre of a face, the centre, start or end of an edge — as a translucent frame. Click the one you want; the chip shows it.
3. Fill in the **Name** (a free `c1`-style default is prefilled). Adjust the frame-local **Offset** x / y / z and click the rotation stepper to turn the frame 90° about its own Z per click.
4. Click **Apply**. The statement is written directly in the part body and the timeline's part row gains a connector under its *N connectors* toggle. In the viewport the connector renders as a small axis triad.

The bracket names the faces a neighbour will touch — `foot` for the surface it stands on, `back` for the beam it hangs from — and the two holes a fastener seats in. In an assembly, `mate('fastened', beam.connectors.side, bracket.connectors.back)` hangs it on the beam.

## Signature

```ts
connector(name, source, options?)
```

| Argument | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| `name` | Identifier, unique within the part. Reached as `instance.connectors.<name>`. |
| `source` | The geometry the frame is derived from — see below. |
| `options.xDirection` | Optional X direction (an axis name or vector), re-orthogonalised against the frame's Z. |

Two chained modifiers move the frame after it is derived, in call order:

| Modifier | Effect |
|----------|--------|
| `.offset(x, y?, z?)` | Translate the origin along the frame's own axes. |
| `.rotate('x' \| 'y' \| 'z', degrees)` | Rotate the frame about one of its own axes, pivoting at its origin. |

## Where the frame comes from

| Source | Origin | Z |
|--------|--------|---|
| A planar face — `select(face().onPlane('xy', 10))` | Face centre | Face normal, pointing out of the solid |
| A cylindrical or conical face — `select(face().cylinder())` | On the axis, at the face's mid-height | The surface axis, always oriented up (or +Y / +X when horizontal), never toward the seam |
| A circular edge — `select(edge().circle())` | Circle centre | Circle axis |
| A straight edge — `select(edge().line())` | Edge midpoint | Edge tangent |
| A vertex | The point | World Z |
| An anchored vertex — `e.endFaces().center()`, `sel.start()`, `sel.end()` | The anchor point | The anchored face or edge's own direction |
| A plane — `plane('xy', { offset: 20 })` | Plane origin | Plane normal |

The source must resolve to exactly one face, edge or vertex. A raw point is refused on purpose: a frame tied to geometry re-derives correctly when the part changes; a hard-coded point would drift.

Face frames follow the outward normal, so two parts mated **face-to-face** end up with their connector Zs pointing at each other — that is the default a [mate](/docs/assembly/introduction) assumes, and `.flip()` on the mate is how you get the other way round.

## Rules

- Declare connectors **directly in the part body**, not inside a `sketch()` or other callback — nested, the connector would register nowhere and the statement is refused.
- Names are unique within a part; a part connector and an assembly connector may share a name.
- Connectors are part-owned. There is no way to add one to a single instance from the assembly; edit the part, or use an assembly-level free frame (`connector('name', [x, y, z])`, see the [assembly introduction](/docs/assembly/introduction#assembly-connectors)).
- The pen button on a connector chip in the mate dialog edits a part connector's name, offset and rotation from the assembly — the edit is written into the part file.

## Copying a connector

A copy of a connector is a connector too — a mate side, a replicate row — with no statement of its own. The copies of `bolt` are addressed `bolt.instance(1)`, `bolt.instance(2)`, …; in these pages `bolt.instance(3)` is *copy 3 of `bolt`*.

<img src="/img/docs/ui/copy-dialog-along-repeat.png" alt="The Copy dialog on Along a repeat: the bolt connector in Solids & connectors, the holes repeat in Pattern" width="240" style={{float: 'right', marginLeft: '1.5rem'}} />

1. Right-click the connector's row in the timeline (under the part's *N connectors* toggle) and choose **Copy…**. The Copy dialog opens with the connector in its **Solids & connectors** slot. Or click **Copy** on the toolbar and click the connector's triad: every connector shows while the dialog is open.
2. Pick the **Type**. **Along a repeat** puts one copy on every instance of a feature you repeated: its **Pattern** slot asks for the repeat — click the Repeat row in the timeline, or the repeated feature in the viewport. Count, spacing and axis come from the repeat, so the dialog hides them. **Linear** and **Circular** lay the copies out on their own, with the fields of a [solid copy](/docs/patterns/copy); their **Axis** slot also takes a connector, for its Z axis.
3. A translucent triad previews each copy. Click **Apply**. The `copy()` statement is written at the end of the part body, and the timeline files the Copy row under the part's connectors; clicking the row highlights the connector with all its copies.

An assembly mates a copy like any connector: `mate('fastened', f.connectors.bolt.instance(3), b.connectors.head)` seats a bolt in the hole three steps round from the first. Dialog chips show the same spelling, `Pipe flange · bolt.instance(3)`.

### How copies behave

- **Read-only.** A copy is not edited, renamed or removed on its own. Change the connector and every copy follows; change the pattern by double-clicking the Copy row. In the mate dialog, the pen on a copy's chip edits the connector it copies, and says so.
- **Numbered like a repeat's instances.** Along a repeat, `bolt.instance(k)` sits on the repeat's instance `k`, and an instance the repeat skips has no copy. A linear grid counts its cells with the first direction varying slowest, the original keeping its own cell (0, or the centre cell when centered); a circular copy counts the steps round from the original at 0. The original's own slot is the connector itself.
- **A moved frame, not a new anchor.** A copy is the connector's frame moved by the pattern. It does not re-attach to the geometry where it lands, so a Linear or Circular copy sits on a hole only while its pattern matches the holes'. **Along a repeat** keeps every copy on its hole when the repeat's count, spacing or axis changes, and it is the way to follow a repeat over a partial arc: a Circular copy steps that arc differently (see [Copy](/docs/patterns/copy#circular-copy)).
- **Renumbering.** A grid's cells are numbered in one sequence, so changing a later direction's count, or the count of a centered pattern, gives the slots new numbers: a part mated to `hole.instance(2)` of a 2 × 2 grid jumps to another hole when the grid becomes 2 × 3. The jump shows in the assembly, so it does not go unnoticed.
- **Limits.** One copy statement per connector; a copy is not copied again (a grid is one Linear copy with two directions); copies are made directly in the part body, never in a sketch. A Circular copy of a connector can't be **Centered** yet.
- **Addresses that don't exist.** A slot the pattern skipped, a slot past the count, or `.instance()` on a connector nothing copies stops the assembly with an error that names the copy statement: `bolt.instance(7) is out of range — the copy at flange.part.js:40 makes instances 0–5`.
