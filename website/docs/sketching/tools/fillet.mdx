# Fillet

A 2D fillet rounds the corner where two sketch edges meet: the edges are trimmed back and a tangent arc fills the gap.

## In the viewport

<img src="/img/docs/ui/sketch-fillet-dialog.png" alt="The Fillet dialog with the four sides of a plate picked, reporting four corners and a radius of 12" width="240" style={{float: 'right', marginLeft: '1.5rem'}} />

1. Click **Fillet** on the sketch toolbar. The Fillet dialog opens docked on the right with a **Selection** slot and a **Radius** field.
2. Click the two edges that share the corner — or every edge whose corners you want rounded. Picks accumulate as chips; click a picked edge again, or a chip's **✕**, to drop it. The dialog counts what it found (*4 corners will be filleted*) and previews the arcs in blue.
3. Type the **Radius** and click **Apply**.

<div style={{clear: 'both'}} />

The tool does not write a `fillet()` call. For each corner it writes a real [`arc()`](/docs/sketching/primitives/arc) plus the constraints that make it a fillet: two `coincident` (the arc's ends onto the trimmed edges), two `tangent`, one `radius` dimension on the first arc and `equal` across the rest. In the same edit, each edge's corner end moves to where the arc touches it and the corner's old coincident is removed.

Constraints that held the corner itself are kept on a [`point()`](/docs/sketching/primitives/point) left at the corner and held on both edges, a *virtual sharp*. This covers a corner pinned to the origin or an axis, a width measured corner to corner, and a rectangle centred through its corners. A rectangle drawn from the origin keeps that corner there, and its width and height still measure the sharp outline. Rounding never changes the shape's size or position. The arc and its radius stay editable afterwards: drag the arc or change the radius label.

![A mounting plate with rounded corners](/img/docs/sketching/tool-fillet.png)

By hand the derived form is shorter: one `fillet(radius, ...lines)` call rounds every corner the listed lines share. It is written after the constraints, like every derived operation.
</ExplainedCode>

## By hand

```js
fillet(radius, l1, l2)             // one corner
fillet(radius, l1, l2, l3, l4)     // every corner the lines share
```

Outside a sketch, `fillet()` rounds the edges of solids — the 3D counterpart on the [Fillet](/docs/3d-operations/fillet) page.
