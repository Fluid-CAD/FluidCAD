# Mirror

Mirror reflects sketch geometry across a line or an axis. Draw one half of a symmetric profile, mirror it, and the two halves join into one region.

## In the viewport

<img src="/img/docs/ui/sketch-mirror-dialog.png" alt="The Mirror dialog with a latch plate's three edges picked and the sketch Y axis as the mirror line" width="240" style={{float: 'right', marginLeft: '1.5rem'}} />

1. Click **Mirror** on the sketch toolbar (shortcut **M**). The Mirror dialog opens docked on the right with a **Geometry** slot and a **Mirror line** slot.
2. Click the sketch edges to mirror. Picks accumulate as chips; click a picked edge again, or a chip's **✕**, to drop it. Any edge stands for its whole primitive — one side of a rectangle picks the rectangle.
3. Click the **Mirror line** slot, then click a sketched line in the viewport (a [guide](/docs/sketching/tools/guides) works too), or the sketch's own X or Y axis line. An axis pick shows as the chip *Sketch Y axis* and is written as `xAxis()` / `yAxis()`. The reflection previews in blue, and the statement about to be written shows under the dialog.
4. Click **Apply**.

<div style={{clear: 'both'}} />

Double-click the mirror's row in the timeline to reopen the dialog and re-pick the geometry or the line.

## By hand

```js
mirror(line)                            // mirror everything across a sketched line
mirror(line, g1, g2, ...)               // mirror specific geometry across a line
mirror(yAxis())                         // mirror across the sketch's own Y axis
mirror(xAxis(), g1, g2, ...)            // mirror specific geometry across the sketch's X axis
mirror("y", g1, g2, ...)                // across a WORLD axis — see below
```

Any sketched line can be the mirror line, including a [guide](/docs/sketching/tools/guides) you do not want in the profile. Write the statement after the constraints — mirror consumes solved geometry.

## Mirrored geometry and constraints

The mirrored copies are solver entities in their own right, rigidly tied to their sources across the mirror line: they move when the source moves, and constraining a copy drives the source. When the mirror line is a sketched line, the tie follows it too — constraining a copy can move the line. In the viewport, pick a mirrored edge or one of its vertices exactly like drawn geometry; the constraint bar and the drawing tools' snaps write the statement for you.

By hand, address a copy with `m.instance(source)` — the mirrored statement, never one of its points:

```js
const m = mirror(yAxis(), l, c)
coincident(m.instance(l).start(), p)        // the copy's vertex, tied back to l
radius(m.instance(c), 8)                    // dimensions the source circle too
```

`m.instance(cp.instance(2))` addresses the mirror image of a [copy](/docs/sketching/tools/copy#copies-and-constraints) instance, and `m.instance(l)` is also a whole-geometry operand (`offset(2, m.instance(l))`). The mirror line itself has no image: constrain it directly. Geometry the mirror does not stamp — written after it, or not among its targets — and geometry without solver identity (an offset result) error as targets.

## Sketch axes and world axes

Bare axis strings like `"x"` and `"y"` always mean **world** axes. On the XY plane that is also the sketch's own X and Y, but on other planes it is not:

```js
// ✗ collapses: world Y is the front plane's normal
sketch("front", () => {
    const b = bezier([200, 200], [-120, 380], [200, 450], [0, 600])
    mirror("y", b)
})
```

The sketch's own axes are the datums `xAxis()` and `yAxis()` — the same fixed lines constraints target (`collinear(xAxis(), l)`), accepted here as the mirror axis:

![The same latch on the front plane, mirrored across local Y](/img/docs/sketching/tool-mirror-local.png)

<ExplainedCode code={toolMirrorLocal} fileName="plate.part.js" />

| Sketch plane | `xAxis()` | `yAxis()` |
|---|---|---|
| `"xy"` / `"top"` | world +X | world +Y |
| `"xz"` / `"front"` | world +X | world +Z |
| `"yz"` / `"right"` | world +Y | world +Z |

The datums work with face sketches and custom `plane()` objects, and they are per argument — bare strings in the same call stay world-relative:

```js
sketch("front", () => {
    copy("linear", [yAxis(), "x"], { count: 2, offset: 30 }, shape)
})
```

You do not need the datums when the mirror line is a sketched line (it already lies on the plane), or on `sketch("xy")`, where world and sketch axes agree.

For mirroring a **feature** rather than sketch geometry, see [Patterns → Repeat](/docs/patterns/repeat#mirror-repeat); for solids, [Transforms → Mirror](/docs/transforms/mirror).
