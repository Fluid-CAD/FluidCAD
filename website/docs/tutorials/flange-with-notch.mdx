# Building a Flange With Notch

<ViewerEmbed code={flangeWithNotchFinal} entry="flange-with-notch.fluid.js" />

In this tutorial, you'll build a flange with a notch, based on the [original design by Too Tall Toby](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4XeLNQvhoyw). You build it entirely from the FluidCAD workspace: the toolbar, the sketch tools, the constraint bar and the feature dialogs. Every dialog writes a line of code into your file as you apply it, so the [full code](#full-code) at the end is what the UI produced, not something you type.

It covers a tangent outline built from a rough sketch and a handful of constraints (Tangent, Concentric, Symmetric and a typed radius), the 2D Mirror tool, sketching on faces the model already has, the centered Rectangle tool, and shallow cuts for a notch and two counterbores.

The drawing gives everything you need: a Ø70 flange with a Ø42 bore, two Ø10 holes 100 apart with R16 tabs around them, a pipe 46 tall, a 30-wide notch 8 deep, and Ø20 counterbores 4 deep.

## Before you start

Open a workspace and create a new part file: click **+** in the top bar and type `flange-with-notch.fluid.js`. The new tab opens on an empty scene. The screenshots use the light theme; the sun/moon button at the top right switches it.

A few UI habits you will use throughout:

- **Ctrl+click** adds an entity to the current selection. A plain click replaces it.
- The **constraint bar** appears under the toolbar while you sketch. Its buttons are icons; hover one for its name. A button lights up only when the current selection makes sense for it.
- **Dimension** is one button. What it writes depends on what you picked: a circle gives a diameter, an arc a radius, a point and an axis a distance.
- The **DOF pill** at the bottom of the viewport counts the degrees of freedom left in a sketch and turns into *Fully constrained* when you are done. Every sketch in this tutorial ends there.
- Every feature dialog shows the **statement** it is about to write, just under the dialog. **Apply** (or <kbd>Enter</kbd>) writes it; **Exit** / **Cancel** (or <kbd>Escape</kbd>) discards it.
- Picking a point that sits under a dimension label picks the label instead. Zoom in with the wheel until the label clears, pick, and zoom back out.

## Step 1: The flange profile

### Start a sketch on the XY plane

Click **Sketch** in the toolbar. The dialog asks for a face or a plane, and the three origin planes appear in the viewport.

![Sketch tool armed, waiting for a plane](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-01-sketch-pick-plane.png)

Click the **XY** plane. The camera locks onto it, the sketch toolbar replaces the feature toolbar, and the constraint bar appears above the viewport.

![An empty sketch on the XY plane](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-02-sketch-xy.png)

### Three circles

Pick **Circle**, click the **origin**, type `70` and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>. Clicking the origin snaps the centre to it, so the circle arrives with a `coincident` to the origin and its `diameter`: *Fully constrained* straight away. Do it again for the bore: click the origin, type `42`.

Now the bolt hole. Click a point on the **horizontal axis** to the right of the circles and type `10`. Snapping to the axis writes `coincident(hole.center(), xAxis())`, which leaves one degree of freedom: where along the axis. Click the hole's **centre**, **Ctrl+click** the **vertical axis**, click **Dimension** and type `50`.

![Three circles: Ø70, Ø42 and the Ø10 hole 50 mm out](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-03-circles.png)

### Rough out one tab

The tab around the hole is two flanks tangent to the outer circle and an R16 cap concentric with the hole. Draw it roughly and let the constraints place it: pick **Line** and draw a flank from the upper part of the outer circle out to the upper right of the hole. Draw a second **Line** below the axis, mirrored by eye. Then pick **3-Pt Arc**, click the **end of the lower line**, the **end of the upper line**, and a third point out past the hole for the bulge. Snapping the arc onto the two line ends writes the two coincidents for free.

Do not try to be accurate. These are guesses; only the shape of the loop matters.

![The rough tab: two lines and an arc, 9 DOF left](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-04-rough-outline.png)

### Say what the tab is

Now the constraint bar, one relation at a time. Watch the DOF pill count down.

1. Click the **upper flank**, **Ctrl+click** the **outer circle**, click **Tangent**. The line swings onto the circle.
2. Click the upper flank's **start point**, **Ctrl+click** the **outer circle**, click **Coincident**. The line now starts exactly at the tangency point.
3. Click the **arc**, **Ctrl+click** the **hole** (pick the hole on its right side, away from its Ø10 label), click **Concentric**.
4. Click the **arc** alone, click **Dimension**, type `16`. On an arc, Dimension writes a `radius`.
5. Click the **upper flank**, **Ctrl+click** the **arc**, click **Tangent**.
6. Click the upper flank's **start point**, **Ctrl+click** the lower flank's **start point**, **Ctrl+click** the **horizontal axis**, click **Symmetric**.
7. Click the **lower flank**, **Ctrl+click** the **arc**, click **Tangent**.

The pill reads *Fully constrained* with nothing redundant. No coordinates were typed for the tab at all: the outer circle, the hole and the R16 decide where the flanks touch down.

![The tab fully constrained: two tangent flanks and the R16 cap](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-05-tab-constrained.png)

A line tangent to two curves could sit on either side of them. The guess decides: the solver keeps each flank on the side you drew it, so drawing it roughly in the right place *is* the choice.

### Mirror the tab

Pick the **Mirror** tool from the sketch toolbar. Click the **upper flank**, the **arc**, the **lower flank** and the **hole** for **Geometry**, then click the **Mirror line** slot and click the **vertical axis**. The blue outline is the mirrored copy and the statement reads `mirror(yAxis(), upper, cap, lower, hole)`.

![The 2D Mirror tool with the mirrored tab preview](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-06-mirror-ghost.png)

Click **Apply**. The profile is complete and still fully constrained; the mirrored half follows the original.

![The complete flange profile](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-07-profile-complete.png)

### Extrude the flange

Click **Finish Sketch ▾** and choose **Extrude**. Set **Distance** to `12`. The green ghost is the live preview, and the statement reads `extrude(12)`. The two inner loops, the bore and the holes, become holes on their own because **Drill holes** is on.

![Extrude dialog with the 12 mm ghost preview](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-08-extrude-ghost.png)

Click **Apply**.

![The extruded flange](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-09-flange.png)

## Step 2: The pipe

### Sketch on the flange's top face

Click **Sketch** and this time click the **top face of the flange**. The chip reads *Face — Extrude*: FluidCAD remembered which operation produced that face, so the sketch follows the flange if you change it later. The section view shows the flange in grey with the origin at the bore's centre.

![A sketch opened on the flange top face](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-10-sketch-face.png)

### Two circles on the origin

Pick **Circle**, click the **origin**, type `70`. Click the origin again and type `42`. Both snap to the origin, so the ring is fully constrained the moment it is drawn.

![The Ø70 / Ø42 ring on the flange top](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-11-ring-sketch.png)

### Extrude the ring

**Finish Sketch ▾ → Extrude**, **Distance** `34`. The flange is 12 tall and the pipe tops out at 46, so the ring grows by 34 from the face it sits on.

![Extrude dialog with the 34 mm pipe ghost](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-12-pipe-ghost.png)

Click **Apply**. The tube fuses onto the flange, sharing its bore.

![The pipe on the flange](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-13-pipe.png)

## Step 3: The notch

### Sketch on the pipe's top face

Tilt the view so you look down on the pipe, click **Sketch**, and click the **top rim of the pipe**. The datum planes also sit in the pick set, so if the chip says *XZ plane* rather than *Face — Extrude*, clear it with **✕** and click the rim from a steeper angle.

![A sketch opened on the pipe top](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-14-sketch-pipe-top.png)

### A centered rectangle

The notch is 30 wide and runs right across the pipe, so it is a 30 × 70 rectangle centered on the origin. Click **Rectangle** and switch on **Centered** in its option menu; the rectangle then grows from its middle instead of from a corner.

![The Rectangle tool's option menu with Centered on](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-15-rectangle-options.png)

Click the **origin**. A **W** readout follows the mouse; type `30` and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>. The readout switches to **H**; type `70` and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>.

![Rectangle tool: the width is typed, the height comes next](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-16-rectangle-size.png)

One gesture writes four lines, their corner coincidents, two horizontals, two verticals, the 30 and 70 dimensions, and, because you clicked the origin, a `midpoint` tying the rectangle's centre to it. *Fully constrained*.

![The 30 × 70 rectangle centered on the pipe](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-17-notch-sketch.png)

### Cut it 8 deep

**Finish Sketch ▾ → Extrude**, switch to the **Remove** tab, **Depth** `8`. The red ghost shows the material about to go; the statement reads `cut(8)`.

![Extrude dialog on the Remove tab with the 8 mm notch ghost](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-18-notch-cut-ghost.png)

Click **Apply**. The rectangle only meets solid where it crosses the pipe wall, so one cut makes both sides of the notch.

![The notched pipe](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-19-notch.png)

## Step 4: Counterbores

### Back to the flange top

Click **Sketch** and click the **flange's top face** again, out on one of the tabs. The chip says *Face — Cut* now, because the last feature to touch the body was the notch, but the statement still names the flange's own end face.

![A sketch on the flange top for the counterbores](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-20-sketch-flange-top.png)

### One Ø20 circle over the hole

Pick **Circle**, click the **centre of the right bolt hole** (the snap finds it), type `20`. The snap only placed the centre, it did not constrain it, so the pill still counts 2 DOF. Zoom in on the hole so its Ø20 label clears the centre, then click the circle's **centre**, **Ctrl+click** the **vertical axis**, **Dimension**, `50`; and again the centre, **Ctrl+click** the **horizontal axis**, **Coincident**. *Fully constrained*.

![The Ø20 counterbore circle, fully constrained](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-21-counterbore-circle.png)

### Mirror it

Pick **Mirror**, click the **circle**, click the **Mirror line** slot and click the **vertical axis**.

![The 2D Mirror tool with the mirrored circle preview](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-22-counterbore-mirror.png)

Click **Apply**. Two counterbores from one constrained circle.

![Both counterbore circles](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-23-counterbore-sketch.png)

### Cut them 4 deep

**Finish Sketch ▾ → Extrude**, **Remove** tab, **Depth** `4`. The statement reads `cut(4)`.

![Extrude dialog with the 4 mm counterbore ghost](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-24-counterbore-cut-ghost.png)

Click **Apply**. That is the flange.

![The finished flange with notch](/img/docs/tutorials/flange-with-notch-ui-25-final.png)

## What you practiced

- **Circle** snapped to the origin or an axis, and **Dimension** against the sketch's own axes instead of typed coordinates
- A **rough outline** (Line, Line, 3-Pt Arc) turned into an exact one by **Tangent**, **Concentric**, a typed **radius** and **Symmetric**, with the DOF pill as the check that nothing is left loose
- The 2D **Mirror** tool, twice, for the second tab and the second counterbore
- **Sketching on a face** an operation produced, and reading the *Face — Extrude* / *Face — Cut* chip
- **Rectangle** with its **Centered** option, one gesture for a fully constrained notch profile
- **Extrude** on the **Remove** tab with a typed depth, for cuts that stop part way through
